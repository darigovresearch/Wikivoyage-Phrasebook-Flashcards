<b id="mwBg">Romanian</b> (<i id="mwBw">limba română</i>) is a Romance language spoken mainly in <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Romania" title="Romania" id="mwCA">Romania</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Moldova" title="Moldova" id="mwCQ">Moldova</a> (where it is sometimes called <b id="mwCg">Moldovan</b>, <i id="mwCw">limba moldovenească</i>) as well as in some parts of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary" id="mwDA">Hungary</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Serbia" title="Serbia" id="mwDQ">Serbia</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Bulgaria" title="Bulgaria" id="mwDg">Bulgaria</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Ukraine" title="Ukraine" id="mwDw">Ukraine</a>. It is useful to know the language if travelling in Romania, especially in rural areas.</p>

<p id="mwEA">Romanian is very similar to Italian, and, to a lesser extent, other Romance languages (<a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/French_phrasebook" title="French phrasebook" id="mwEQ">French</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Spanish_phrasebook" title="Spanish phrasebook" id="mwEg">Spanish</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Portuguese_phrasebook" title="Portuguese phrasebook" id="mwEw">Portuguese</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Catalan_phrasebook" title="Catalan phrasebook" id="mwFA">Catalan</a>, etc.), so speakers of these languages are more at an advantage.</p>

<p id="mwFQ">Note that in Romanian, there is a formal and informal form when addressing people. The informal is <b id="mwFg">tu</b> (you, singular) or <b id="mwFw">voi</b> (you, plural) and the formal is <b id="mwGA">dumneavoastră</b> (for both singular and plural). There is also a formal way of speaking about other people, not just when addressing them. When referring to <b id="mwGQ">she</b> use <b id="mwGg">dumneaei</b>; for <b id="mwGw">he</b> use <b id="mwHA">dumnealui</b>; for <b id="mwHQ">you</b> use <b id="mwHg">dumneata</b>; and for <b id="mwHw">them</b> use <b id="mwIA">dumnealor</b>. Note that the formal form should be used with the <i id="mwIQ">plural</i> form of the verb, at the appropriate person. This is similar to the construction in most other Romance languages, and, to an extent, German, although Germans usually make less use of the informal forms. Use <b id="mwIg">tu</b> when addressing friends or people you know well. When addressing strangers or speaking about strangers, use the formal forms.</p>

<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" id="mwIw" data-mw='{"autoGenerated":true}'/></section><section data-mw-section-id="1" id="mwJA" aria-labelledby="Background"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Background">Background</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Romanian_phrasebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwJQ">Romanian should not be confused with Romani or Romany, which is a language of the European <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Roma_culture_in_Europe" title="Roma culture in Europe" id="mwJg">Roma</a>. The similarity is coincidental; the English word for the European Roma stems from an Indian/Sanskrit root. The name Romania or Rumania and its derivatives come from the Latin word 'Romanus', and are etymologically related to Rome, the capital city of the Roman Empire and now the capital city of Italy.</p>

<p id="mwJw">Romanian tends to be easy for speakers of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Italian_phrasebook" title="Italian phrasebook" id="mwKA">Italian</a>, Spanish, Portuguese and Catalan to learn, since all these languages share a common root and influences and are all written as they are pronounced.  Romanian is sometimes challenging because of the influx of loanwords, mostly Slavic words, as well as a few <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Hungarian_phrasebook" title="Hungarian phrasebook" id="mwKQ">Hungarian</a>, German and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Turkish_phrasebook" title="Turkish phrasebook" id="mwKg">Turkish</a> ones, although most of these have fallen out of use for a very long time. Neologisms have been imported en masse from French, Italian, German, and English. The slang has either French, German or Romani origin. The language uses the Latin alphabet, and the Latinate roots can help tourists to understand some signs, such as Alimentara (a place where you can buy aliments, food) Farmacie (pharmacy, drugstore), and Poliție (police).  Some signs, however, are 'false friends' with English - for example, Librărie means a bookstore rather than a lending library (as it does in French); also, Teatru means a stage theatre and never a cinema. Helpfully, these "false friends" also apply in all of Romanian's sister languages.</p>

<p id="mwKw">The Romanian language is generally seen as a language with somewhat complicated grammar, but far simpler for speakers of Germanic languages than any of its Slavic neighbours or Hungarian. Romanian has a phonetic alphabet, so a person can look at a word and know how it is pronounced. Romanian is abundant in vowels, and it can have series of diphthongs and even triphthongs which give the language a melodious and musical sound.</p>

<p id="mwLA">A foreigner trying to learn or speak Romanian can expect mostly positive reactions from native speakers. Most Romanian people will certainly love you for it and will greatly appreciate the fact that you are making an effort to speak their language. Others, however, may sneer at you for not properly being able to speak what they see as a very easy phonetic language. The Romanian alphabet is nearly exactly the same as the English alphabet, except for five additional accented letters, or 'diacriticals': ă, ș, ț, â, and î. </p>

<p id="mwLQ">English has effectively become a requirement for getting a somewhat better job, and it is usually the second most popular non-Romance language spoken (on par with German). Consequently, it is usually good to ask before starting to speak English, but more often than not it will be safe to go ahead. Other Romance languages (especially French, Spanish and Italian) are also learnt by most people in schools and are therefore spoken with a pretty good level of fluency by many people, but make sure you ask before you start speaking to people in foreign tongues. In general, Romanians prefer speaking other Romance languages than the others. Globalization and the fact that Romania joined the Latin Union (Uniunea Latină), a linguistic association created in 1954, has made these closely-related languages more attractive.</p>

<p id="mwLg">Some people feel that Romanian is an extremely easy language to learn if you already speak a Latin-based language. This is especially so in contrast to Romania's Slavic and Hungarian neighbors. However, speakers of Slavic languages will find quite a few familiar words, especially in the more basic vocabulary of Romanian, such as "trebuie" ("need" - cf. Polish "trzeba"); "iubi" (love - cf. Czech lubi) etc.</p>

</section><section data-mw-section-id="9" id="mwpw" aria-labelledby="Case"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Case">Case</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Romanian_phrasebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Case"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwqA">Romanian has five grammatical cases: nominative, accusative, genitive, dative, and vocative. </p>

<p id="mwqQ">Don't let this intimidate you; the nominative and the accusative cases are interchangeable with one another, and the same thing also applies to the genitive and dative cases. The vocative is only used in informal conversations.</p>

</section></section><section data-mw-section-id="10" id="mw0A" aria-labelledby="Pronunciation_Guide"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Pronunciation_Guide">Pronunciation Guide</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Romanian_phrasebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Pronunciation Guide"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mw0Q">Romanian pronunciation is <b id="mw0g">very</b> phonetic.  The accent and sounds are almost identical to Italian and other Romance languages (with very few, if any, Slavic influences), so remember to sound every letter clearly. Also, sounds very rarely differ between words (e.g. the letter <b id="mw0w">i</b> is always pronounced the same, every time, unlike in English or even French).</p>

<p id="mw1A">Like English, Romanian has secondary stresses in words. We have not attempted to represent those here. Stress usually falls on the second-last syllable if it ends in a vowel, and last if it ends in a consonant. If you know another Romance language, you shouldn't worry as the stress patterns are usually the same in similar-sounding words.</p>

<p id="mw1Q">Questions in Romanian that end with a verb often use a rising tone on the last syllable or two.</p>

<section data-mw-section-id="20" id="mwArk" aria-labelledby="Clock_time"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading4"><h4 id="Clock_time">Clock time</h4><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Romanian_phrasebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=20" title="Edit section: Clock time"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>

<p id="mwAro">Although 12-hour clocks are common in Romania, time is almost always stated according to the 24-hour clock. </p>

<dl id="mwArs"><dt id="mwArw">one o'clock AM</dt><dd id="mwAr0">ora unu (<i id="mwAr4">OHR-ah OO-noo</i>)</dd>
