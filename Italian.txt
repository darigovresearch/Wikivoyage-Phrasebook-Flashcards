<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb" id="mwBg"><a href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/File:Map_Italophone_World_-_updated.png" class="mw-file-description" id="mwBw"><img resource="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/File:Map_Italophone_World_-_updated.png" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Map_Italophone_World_-_updated.png/500px-Map_Italophone_World_-_updated.png?utm_source=en.wikivoyage.org&amp;utm_campaign=parser&amp;utm_content=thumbnail" decoding="async" data-file-width="1357" data-file-height="628" data-file-type="bitmap" height="231" width="500" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Map_Italophone_World_-_updated.png/1280px-Map_Italophone_World_-_updated.png?utm_source=en.wikivoyage.org&amp;utm_campaign=parser&amp;utm_content=thumbnail 2x" class="mw-file-element" id="mwCA"/></a><figcaption id="mwCQ">Italian speaking areas - Dark blue: native language, Light blue: former Italian colonies, Green square: Italophone minorities</figcaption></figure>
<p id="mwCg"><b id="mwCw">Italian</b> (<i id="mwDA">italiano</i>) is the official language of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Italy" title="Italy" id="mwDQ">Italy</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/San_Marino" title="San Marino" id="mwDg">San Marino</a>, and one of the four official languages of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland" id="mwDw">Switzerland</a>, spoken mainly in the cantons of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Ticino" title="Ticino" id="mwEA">Ticino</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Graubünden" title="Graubünden" id="mwEQ">Graubünden</a> (Grigioni). It is the primary language of the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Rome/Vatican" title="Rome/Vatican" id="mwEg">Vatican City</a>, where it is co-official with Latin, and a co-official language in <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Istria" title="Istria" id="mwEw">Istria</a>, a coastal region of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Slovenia" title="Slovenia" id="mwFA">Slovenia</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Croatia" title="Croatia" id="mwFQ">Croatia</a>. Furthermore, it is understood by a sizable minority in <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Malta" title="Malta" id="mwFg">Malta</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Monaco" title="Monaco" id="mwFw">Monaco</a>, the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/France" title="France" id="mwGA">French</a> island of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Corsica" title="Corsica" id="mwGQ">Corsica</a>, and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Albania" title="Albania" id="mwGg">Albania</a>. Outside of Europe, remnants of Italian are found in <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Libya" title="Libya" id="mwGw">Libya</a>, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Eritrea" title="Eritrea" id="mwHA">Eritrea</a>, and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Somalia" title="Somalia" id="mwHQ">Somalia</a>.</p>

<p id="mwHg">In most of the touristy resorts along Italy's coasts, English and German are widely spoken, but in any part of Italy, it will hold you in good stead to know at least basic phrases of polite Italian and not to assume that whoever you are speaking with will know your language.</p>

<p id="mwHw">Italian is the most important language in <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/European_classical_music" title="European classical music" id="mwIA">classical music</a>, with most operas being written in Italian, and musical notation on scores being primarily in Italian. All musicians who play Western music are required to be familiar with Italian musical terminology, and it was <i id="mwIQ">de rigueur</i> for European composers from the Renaissance to the first half of the 20th century to have at least some basic knowledge of Italian.</p>

<p id="mwIg">If you speak Spanish or even Portuguese, be careful as there are a lot of words that look and sound almost the same in these languages but convey very different meanings: for example,  Spanish <i id="mwIw">guardar</i> ("to store, shelve") vs. Italian <i id="mwJA">guardare</i> ("to see, watch"), Spanish <i id="mwJQ">salir</i> ("to get out") vs. Italian <i id="mwJg">salire</i> ("to go up"), Spanish <i id="mwJw">burro</i> ("donkey") vs. Italian <i id="mwKA">burro</i> ("butter"), Spanish <i id="mwKQ">caldo</i> ("soup") vs. Italian <i id="mwKg">caldo</i> ("hot"), Spanish <i id="mwKw">aceite</i> ("oil") vs Italian <i id="mwLA">aceto</i> ("vinegar") and Spanish <i id="mwLQ">sembrar</i> ("to sow, plant") vs Italian <i id="mwLg">sembrare</i> ("to seem").</p>

<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" id="mwLw" data-mw='{"autoGenerated":true}'/></section><section data-mw-section-id="1" id="mwMA" aria-labelledby="Grammar"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Grammar">Grammar</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Italian_phrasebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Grammar"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>

<section data-mw-section-id="2" id="mwMQ" aria-labelledby="Nouns"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Nouns">Nouns</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Italian_phrasebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Nouns"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwMg">All Italian nouns are assigned one of two genders: masculine or feminine. Unlike in English, even inanimate objects are assigned a gender (e.g. <i id="mwMw">tavolo</i> [table] is masculine, <i id="mwNA">casa</i> [house] is feminine). The article before a noun depends on its gender: e.g. <i id="mwNQ">il</i> (m for most cases), <i id="mwNg">lo</i> (m if word starts with <i id="mwNw">s + consonant</i>, z, gn, ps, pn, x, y and consonant clusters), <i id="mwOA">la</i> (f for most cases) or <i id="mwOQ">l'</i> (if word starts with a vowel). Likewise, third person subject pronouns also depend on the grammatical gender of the subject<span typeof="mw:DisplaySpace" id="mwOg"> </span>: <i id="mwOw">lui/egli/esso</i> (m) and <i id="mwPA">lei/ella/essa</i> (f).</p>

</section><section data-mw-section-id="3" id="mwPQ" aria-labelledby="Adjectives"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Adjectives">Adjectives</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Italian_phrasebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Adjectives"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwPg">In Italian, adjectives must agree in gender and number with the noun or pronoun they modify. So for example, the word for "child" in Italian is <i id="mwPw">bambino</i> (masculine), plural <i id="mwQA">bambini</i>, or <i id="mwQQ">bambina</i> (feminine), plural <i id="mwQg">bambine</i>. If you want to say "a small boy", you'd use <i id="mwQw">un piccolo bambino</i>, whereas two small boys would be <i id="mwRA">due piccoli bambini</i>, a small girl would be <i id="mwRQ">una piccola bambina</i> and two small girls would be <i id="mwRg">due piccole bambine</i>. If a group of children you are speaking of includes both boys and girls, use the masculine plural form. Unlike English, Italian has no capitalized adjectives, so, for example, "Italian" is always represented in lowercase in Italian, but again must agree in gender and number: <i id="mwRw">un bambino italiano</i>; <i id="mwSA">una bambina italiana</i>.</p>

</section><section data-mw-section-id="4" id="mwSQ" aria-labelledby="Formal_and_familiar_pronouns"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Formal_and_familiar_pronouns">Formal and familiar pronouns</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Italian_phrasebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Formal and familiar pronouns"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwSg">In Italian, you use the polite form of you ("lei") with all the people you don't know; it doesn't matter whether they are older or younger than you, unless they are children. You'll use the familiar form ("tu") with the people you know and with children. The two forms differ in that 2nd person singular verb forms are used for "tu" and 3rd person singular verb forms are used for "lei" (which can also mean "she").</p>

</section><section data-mw-section-id="5" id="mwSw" aria-labelledby="Addressing_people"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Addressing_people">Addressing people</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Italian_phrasebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Addressing people"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwTA">The formal way to address a man you meet for the first time is <i id="mwTQ">signore</i>, while the equivalent when addressing a woman is <i id="mwTg">signora</i>. <i id="mwTw">Signorina</i> may sometimes be used to address a very young, unmarried woman, but unless you are absolutely sure, it is best to default to <i id="mwUA">signora</i>. The corresponding plurals are <i id="mwUQ">signori</i> (all-male and mixed male and female groups) and, somewhat confusingly, <i id="mwUg">signore</i> (all-female groups). The Italian equivalent for the salutation "ladies and gentlemen" is thus "<i id="mwUw">signore e signori</i>". In informal situations, these terms are frequently dispensed with.</p>

</section><section data-mw-section-id="6" id="mwVA" aria-labelledby="Possessives"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Possessives">Possessives</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Italian_phrasebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Possessives"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwVQ">English speakers are used to using possessive pronouns before nouns, as in "my book" or "your car". In Italian, things are usually a bit more complicated. With the exception of individual family members (<i id="mwVg">mia moglie</i> is "my wife"; <i id="mwVw">suo figlio</i> is "his/her son"), these kinds of phrases are replaced by <b id="mwWA">article + possessive pronoun + noun</b>, and both the article and the pronoun must agree in gender and number with the noun being modified. For example, "my book" would be <i id="mwWQ">il mio libro</i>; "your car" would be <i id="mwWg">la tua macchina</i>; "our houses" would be <i id="mwWw">le nostre case</i>. If you leave out the article, however, people will still understand you.</p>

</section></section><section data-mw-section-id="7" id="mwXA" aria-labelledby="Pronunciation"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Pronunciation">Pronunciation</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Italian_phrasebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Pronunciation"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwXQ">Pronunciation is relatively easy in Italian since most words are pronounced exactly how they are written.  The main letters to watch out for are <b id="mwXg">c</b> and <b id="mwXw">g</b>, since their pronunciation varies based on the following vowel. Also, make sure to pronounce <b id="mwYA">r</b> and <b id="mwYQ">rr</b> differently: <i id="mwYg">caro</i> means dear, whereas <i id="mwYw">carro</i> means chariot.</p>

</section><section data-mw-section-id="12" id="mw1w" aria-labelledby="Emphasis"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Emphasis">Emphasis</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Italian_phrasebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Emphasis"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mw2A">Accented words have the emphasis on the syllable with the accented letter. Unaccented words are normally pronounced with the emphasis on the penultimate or antepenultimate syllable.</p>

</section></section><section data-mw-section-id="13" id="mw2Q" aria-labelledby="Cognates_in_English"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Cognates_in_English">Cognates in English</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Italian_phrasebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Cognates in English"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mw2g">English-speakers will find many Italian words easy to understand, if they know Latin-derived equivalents in English. For example, words that end in -tion in English as a rule end in -<span typeof="mw:Entity" id="mw2w">⁠</span>zione in Italian. So Italian <i id="mw3A">introduzione</i> is English "introduction", <i id="mw3Q">preparazione</i> is "preparation" and <i id="mw3g">riduzione</i> is "reduction". In addition, words that end in -ity in English as a rule end in -ità in Italian. So <i id="mw3w">università</i> is "university", <i id="mw4A">unità</i> is "unity", <i id="mw4Q">rapidità</i> is "rapidity" and <i id="mw4g">conduttività</i> is "conductivity", although the respective Italian words for "majority" and "minority" are <i id="mw4w">maggioranza</i> and <i id="mw5A">minoranza</i>, and similarly, <i id="mw5Q">sicurezza</i> is the common word for "security" or "safety". Another signpost to look for is that words that end in -ous in English usually end in -oso as masculine singular adjectives in Italian (see <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="#Adjectives" class="mw-selflink-fragment" id="mw5g">#Adjectives</a> above for other forms), for example <i id="mw5w">generoso</i> (generous), and <i id="mw6A">armonioso</i> (harmonious), but perilous (dangerous) is <i id="mw6Q">pericoloso</i>. Moreover, the meanings can sometimes have diverged between English and Italian, so Italian <i id="mw6g">ricordare</i>, while cognate with English record, actually means "remember". So guessing the Italian word from the English Latin-derived equivalent doesn't always work, but it is still a good idea to try it in conversations.</p>

<p id="mw6w">There are also some Italian words that were borrowed directly into English, but in some cases the meeting has shifted somewhat from the original Italian. For example, <i id="mw7A">vendetta</i> means revenge and <i id="mw7Q">donna</i> means woman (hence <i id="mw7g">prima donna</i>, which literally translates as "first woman") in Italian. When it comes to culinary terms, the Italian original is often a more generic term than the one borrowed into English. For instance, <i id="mw7w">prosciutto</i> refers to any kind of ham and <i id="mw8A">gelato</i> refers to any kind of ice cream in Italian. Be careful, though, as the meanings can sometimes be drastically different. For example, <i id="mw8Q">latte</i> means milk in Italian, so if you want coffee with milk, you need to ask for <i id="mw8g">caffè latte</i>. </p>

<section data-mw-section-id="14" id="mw8w" aria-labelledby="False_friends"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="False_friends">False friends</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Italian_phrasebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: False friends"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mw9A">One serious false friend is <i id="mw9Q">accidenti</i>, which means "Damn!", rather than "accidents", and the singular <i id="mw9g">accidente</i> can also mean "damn", so if you want to describe an accident, use <i id="mw9w">incidente</i>, plural <i id="mw-A">incidenti</i>. Also, be careful to use the word <i id="mw-Q">preservativo</i> correctly: it means a condom, not a preservative! Preservatives, such as are used in food, are <i id="mw-g">conservanti</i>. The word <i id="mw-w">tipica</i> is used in the expression <i id="mw_A">cucina tipica</i>, often mistranslated as "typical cuisine," but it really means "authentic local cuisine". However, if you want to get the English sense of "typical" across, there are other words that you can consider, such as <i id="mw_Q">normale</i> (normal), <i id="mw_g">solito</i> (usual) or <i id="mw_w">ordinario</i> (ordinary).</p>

</section><section data-mw-section-id="24" id="mwAzc" aria-labelledby="Writing_time_and_date"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading4"><h4 id="Writing_time_and_date">Writing time and date</h4><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Italian_phrasebook&amp;action=edit&amp;section=24" title="Edit section: Writing time and date"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwAzg">Italy uses the 24 hour clock.</p>

<p id="mwAzk">First in dates is the day, then is the month, and last is the year.</p>

<p id="mwAzo">For example: 23/1/2010 is in letters <i id="mwAzs">23 gennaio 2010</i>.</p>

<p id="mwAzw">To write clock times, the hour is before the minutes and they are separated by "e" (and).</p>

<p id="mwAz0">For example: 6:43 (written 6.43 in Italy) is read <i id="mwAz4">sei e quarantatre</i>.</p>

<p id="mwAz8">In informal speech, Italians can use a more complex system with a 12 hours clock and words meaning "a quarter past/to" (e.g., 2.45 is <i id="mwA0A">le tre, meno un quarto</i>), but you won't find it written anywhere and everyone understands the 24-hour system. Only remember that you must mention the part of the day (mattina, pomeriggio etc.) instead of saying "am" and "pm".</p>
